Backyard Honey vs. Supermarket Honey

Much of the honey on supermarket shelves is ultra-filtered, pasteurised under high heat, or blended with cheap imported syrups. Here is how our raw local honey compares.

Quality Factor
Backyard Honey
Commercial Supermarket Honey
Pasteurisation & Heat Unheated / Raw. Maintains all natural vitamins, minerals, and live enzymes. Flash-Heated. High temperatures destroy beneficial enzymes and floral flavor.

Heritage & Tradition

8,000+ Years of Beekeeping History

Honey is one of nature’s greatest foods—a natural sweet treasure that never spoils when pure. Discover humanity’s long bond with the honeybee.

Ancient Origins

Stone Age Cave Murals

8,000-year-old cave paintings in Valencia show early humans gathering wild honeycomb. Honey was prized as divine food long before refined sugars existed.

Royal Heritage

Royal Hives & King Charles

Beekeeping has long been a British royal passion. King Charles III keeps organic beehives at Highgrove and Clarence House, championing local bee conservation across the UK.

Traditional Skeps

Straw Skeps to Modern Hives

From woven straw skeps in medieval gardens to modern moveable frame hives, beekeeping evolved to protect the colony and harvest honey cleanly.

Local Commitment

Backyard Honey Ethics

We carry on traditional, sustainable practices. Our local hives support ecosystem pollination, fruit tree flowering, and bio-diversity across local gardens.

Inside The Colony

Hive Anatomy & Roles

Every jar of Backyard Honey is created through extraordinary team effort inside the hive colony.

The Queen Bee

Lays up to 2,000 eggs a day

The heart of the colony. She lives up to 3–5 years and emits natural pheromones that maintain hive harmony and direct worker activity.

Worker Bees

All Female • Foragers & Builders

They forage nectar and pollen, construct beeswax comb, tend to brood, defend the hive entrance, and refine nectar into pure golden honey.

Drone Bees

Male Bees

Drones focus on mating with young queens from neighbouring areas, ensuring healthy genetic diversity and colony strength for future generations.

Has Your Honey Turned Solid or Thick?

Don't panic—this is crystallization, and it is 100% natural proof that your honey is raw and unheated! Natural glucose sets over time around pollen particles.

Never microwave raw honey Place jar in warm water (40°C) Gently stir to re-liquefy
